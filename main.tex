\begin{abstract}
Embodied agents are often evaluated after receiving a task, or after a fixed exploration budget has elapsed.
Long-lived service agents face a different regime: while waiting, they may explore an unfamiliar environment, but the next request is unknown and can arrive during an ongoing action.
The useful question is therefore not what the agent will know at the end of exploration, but what it can reliably do if interrupted \emph{now}.
We formalize this setting as \emph{interruptible pre-task exploration} (\taskname), where a hidden task is drawn from a known service distribution and arrives according to a time-varying hazard.
We introduce \method{}, a counterfactual anytime-readiness agent that learns a joint evidence--time model (\jet{}) from action-prefix branches.
\jet{} predicts when task-reusable evidence becomes reliable within each macro-action; task requirement programs compose these predictions into readiness curves, and an arrival-aware semi-Markov planner selects actions by expected downstream utility at the interruption time.
We further specify \bench{} across exact controlled worlds, large-scale ProcTHOR houses, scanned HM3D environments, high-quality HSSD interiors, photorealistic Habitat-GS scenes, dynamic HSSD histories through P4D-Bench, and indoor/outdoor deployment on three commercial robots. The suite connects HM3D-OVON, OpenEQA, ObjectNav, PointNav, and P4D-Bench to navigation, attribute and spatial question answering, and presence/absence verification under a common hidden-task interruption protocol.
Unlike coverage or endpoint information gain, the proposed objective directly values the timing, reliability, and compositional sufficiency of evidence for downstream service.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{Ethics statement}
The simulated benchmark uses licensed indoor scans, while the physical evaluation also records robot observations in real indoor and outdoor scenes.
Such systems can capture people or private attributes of spaces and can fail unevenly across environments, object categories, and visual conditions.
Physical trials should obtain site permission and use data minimization, access controls, visible recording indicators, and calibrated abstention.
The presence/absence protocol explicitly includes an \textsc{Insufficient} response to discourage unsupported negative claims.
We will document dataset licenses, perception biases, and failure cases with the released benchmark.

\subsection*{Reproducibility statement}

\Cref{sec:method} gives the model, losses, and planning objective, while \cref{sec:experiments} pre-registers baselines, metrics, splits, and statistical comparisons.
The appendix provides pseudocode, the censored likelihood, an endpoint-insufficiency proof, task-program definitions, and a reporting checklist.
