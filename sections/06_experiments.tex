\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We test four hypotheses:
\textbf{H1} endpoint proxies can rank actions differently from AWU;
\textbf{H2} evidence timing improves decisions under interruption;
\textbf{H3} evidence-program factorization improves held-out composition generalization; and
\textbf{H4} endpoint planning degrades as macro-action duration variance grows.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Task-hidden baselines include Random, Nearest Frontier, Coverage, information gain, Semantic Novelty, Explore-then-Act/ECC
\citep{ye2026look}, belief uncertainty
\citep{zhang2026theory}, semantic-affordance priority
\citep{chen2026abot}, and latent-fact coverage
\citep{cai2026autocontext}.
Task-distribution-aware baselines are Expected Requirement Deficit, Successor Features/GPI
\citep{barreto2017successor}, myopic endpoint utility, and model-based reward-free exploration
\citep{sekar2020plan2explore,zhang2021rewardfree}.
Ablations replace \method{} with a Direct Utility Critic, endpoint-only \jet{}, uniform prefix weighting, independent evidence times, or uncertified detections.
Task-Known/ActiveVOO-style
\citep{liu2025activevoo}, Arrival-Known, Oracle Memory, and Hindsight Candidate are reported separately as upper bounds.
Complete adaptations appear in \cref{app:baselines}.

\paragraph{Primary metrics.}
Recall-AWU evaluates $B=0$; Service-AWU permits a short post-arrival budget; Worst-Family Service-AWU is the minimum macro-average across the four task families.
Secondary metrics include readiness area, post-arrival success/cost/SPL, QA accuracy, evidence grounding precision, absence risk--coverage, ECE/Brier, hitting-time calibration, C-index, blockage, latency, and memory.
Coverage is diagnostic only.

\paragraph{Protocol and statistics.}
All methods share candidates, sensors, perception, memory, local control, and downstream executors.
We use at least five training seeds and paired episodes with identical scene, start, task, arrival, and noise seeds.
A hierarchical bootstrap resamples scene, episode, then task/arrival draw.
We report means, 95\% confidence intervals, and paired effects.
Validation scenes select thresholds, robust radius, and early stopping; the test split is run once.
The pre-registered primary comparison is \method{} against the strongest validation-selected task-hidden baseline.

\paragraph{Cross-environment protocol.}
We evaluate the complete agent on ProcTHOR \taskname{} compositions, HM3D-OVON, the active-exploration subset of OpenEQA, HSSD ObjectNav, Habitat-GS PointNav, and P4D-Bench on dynamic HSSD (\cref{tab:evaluation-matrix}).
Each benchmark keeps its official split, goal specification, action interface, and success rule, then adds the \taskname{} wrapper: the goal is hidden before arrival, the declared task prior is shared by all methods, and evaluation starts from the exact interrupted prefix.
Because the tasks and renderers differ, metrics are reported per benchmark rather than averaged across columns.
ProcTHOR supplies the full four-family composition analysis; the remaining domains test transfer across open-vocabulary navigation, natural embodied questions, object and point goals, high-fidelity rendering, and evolving-scene settings.

\paragraph{Real-world protocol.}
Indoor/outdoor trials use the same hidden-task catalog and arrival processes.
An arrival cancels the remaining macro-action after the current observation commit and passes the frozen memory and physical pose to the executor.
Methods receive matched starts, draws, budgets, and platform interfaces; native safety control remains active.
M20 is the primary quantitative platform; X30 and Lite3 evaluate transfer of the unchanged high-level policy across wheel-legged and quadrupedal embodiments.
We report AWU, success, distance, time, interruption prefix, and intervention rate separately by indoor/outdoor environment and platform.

\paragraph{Stress tests.}
We vary arrival family and misspecification, task-prior shift, perception and odometry noise, action-duration variance, joint rank, prefix-bin width, and counterfactual data volume.
A logged single-action variant measures dependence on simulator cloning.
Compute reporting includes branch generation, training, inference latency, and peak memory.
