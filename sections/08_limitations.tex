\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We study static environments, declared task families, and specified arrival hazards, not dynamic-world tracking or arbitrary open-world requests.
Gold requirement programs isolate exploration; natural-language deployment inherits parser errors.
Counterfactual branching is expensive and may be unavailable on robots, so we report its cost and a logged single-action variant.
\method{} cannot recover candidates omitted by the shared generator and remains exposed to perception, localization, and control failure.
Synthetic hazards are system assumptions rather than estimates of human behavior.
Our three-platform deployment spans one manufacturer and does not establish unrestricted longitudinal or cross-stack transfer.
Accordingly, we claim neither a universal agent nor the first task-agnostic explorer; the contribution is evidence-time planning when a hidden request can interrupt any macro-action prefix.
